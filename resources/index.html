📚 Curated Resources

Key Organizations & Research Centers

  • AI Now Institute — Research on AI’s societal impacts
  • Partnership on AI — Collaborative platform for responsible AI
  • Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) — AI policy and safety
  • Stanford Internet Observatory — Tech policy research
  • Data & Society — Social implications of technology
  • Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) — Digital rights nonprofit; privacy, free expression, and surveillance litigation

Books

  • Weapons of Math Destruction — Cathy O’Neil, on how scoring algorithms in finance, policing, and education can quietly encode and amplify inequity
  • Automating Inequality — Virginia Eubanks, on algorithmic decision-making in the American welfare system
  • Race After Technology — Ruha Benjamin, on how bias gets built into tools that look neutral on the surface
  • Algorithms of Oppression — Safiya Umoja Noble, on how search engines reproduce racism and sexism
  • Atlas of AI — Kate Crawford, on the labor, data, and natural resources an AI system actually costs
  • Artificial Unintelligence: How Computers Misunderstand the World — Meredith Broussard, a data journalist’s case against “technochauvinism”
  • Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans — Melanie Mitchell, the clearest plain-language account of how these systems actually work (and don’t)
  • The Age of Surveillance Capitalism — Shoshana Zuboff, on data extraction as a business model
  • The Ethics of Artificial Intelligence: Principles, Challenges, and Opportunities — Luciano Floridi (Oxford University Press), a short, structured overview of the ethical questions AI raises
  • The Internet Con: How to Seize the Means of Computation — Cory Doctorow, on monopoly power, platforms, and who actually controls the infrastructure AI runs on
  • The Reverse Centaur’s Guide to Life After AI - Cory Doctorow, on what happens next with AI and what we can do about it
  • Teaching Machines: The History of Personalized Learning — Audrey Watters, a history of ed-tech’s decades-old promises, well before Silicon Valley showed up

Podcasts

  • Your Undivided Attention — Center for Humane Technology; how technology is designed to capture attention, and what that design costs us
  • Tech Won’t Save Us — Paris Marx; a skeptical, historically grounded look at tech-industry narratives
  • This Machine Kills — Jathan Sadowski and Edward Ongweso Jr.; the political economy of technology
  • In Machines We Trust — MIT Technology Review, hosted by Jennifer Strong; AI’s everyday, on-the-ground impact
  • Better Offline — Ed Zitron; a critical read on the AI industry and its hype cycle
  • Kill Switch — iHeartPodcasts/Kaleidoscope, hosted by Dexter Thomas; a plain-language guide to the tech and algorithms running our lives
  • The 404 Media Podcast — the reporters behind 404 Media on the stories they’re breaking that week

News & Commentary

  • 404 Media — independent, journalist-owned outlet covering hacking, surveillance, AI, and platform power
  • MIT Technology Review — AI
  • The Verge — AI Coverage
  • Wired — AI

Tools & Platforms for Exploration

  • TensorFlow Playground — Interactive ML learning
  • Fast.ai — Free deep learning courses
  • Kaggle — Datasets and competitions

Additional resources will be shared throughout the course. Check D2L for week-specific readings and materials.
