# What We Looked For

A case meets the standard when:

- Every **required section** is there (including Similar Cases and the Group Reflection)
- Every **DTPA section and pillar** makes specific claims **with sources**
- The case is **one specific, documented deployment**, not a trend

The habit we're trying to "train": **a specific claim with a source.**

# What Went Well

Data and Quantitative Literacy were strong across most groups. Some moves we saw:

- Turning a percentage into people: *"99.6% specificity" still means about 4 in 10 positive results are false alarms*
- Testing a company's number against an independent one: *a claimed 90% cost cut vs. 5–10% productivity gains*
- Noticing that **a mean hides the worst case**
- Saying clearly **what you looked for and couldn't find**

---

# Pattern 1: Every Claim Needs a Source You Can Point To

- Put **in-text citations** (Author, Year) on every specific fact and number.
- Every source cited in the text belongs in your **reference list**.
- **Check your sources.** Does it actually say what you claim? For example:
    - We found some claims attributed to sources that don't contain them. **AI tools can mask this!**
    - We found a statistic about one product credited to a different product.

# Pattern 2: Practices and Actions Were the Thinnest

<div class="columns">
<div class="column">

### Practices: critique vs. constraint

- **Critique:** lawsuits, reporting, union statements, research
- **Constraint:** laws, rules, contracts, certifications that actually limit the system
- Did the constraint **change anything**? If you can't tell, say so.

</div>
<div class="column">

### Actions: who decided?

- Who made the decision to deploy it?
- Who **could have changed or stopped it**, and what did they do?
- Is there a **remedy** for people harmed? What would it miss?

</div>
</div>

**It's possible these might be the hardest to research; that's ok.** 

Say what you know; use similar cases to make inferences; avoid generalizing.

---

# Pattern 3: Who Is Behind Your Source?

- Is it **reporting**, **research**, an **official record**, **advocacy**, or the **organization's own account**?
- Watch for **commercial third parties**: a company that sells a tool to get around AI detectors has a reason to say the detector fails.
- Keep **claims** separate from **findings**:
    - *"The lawsuit alleges…"* is not the same as *"The company did…"*
- Watch small overstatements: "only," "first," "has proven," "up to $3 million"

---

# Pattern 4: The Second Half Got Thinner

- **Ethical and Critical Literacy** were often general and uncited.
- A pillar should **reflect across** your DTPA analysis, not repeat one section.
- **Critical Literacy** asks you to *examine* a narrative ("AI is inevitable"), not adopt it. **Who benefits** from that story?
- **Record your disagreements:** both positions, and whether you resolved them.
