# Pattern 1: The Deployment Is the Case

Many of you found great official records. *Many of those are lawsuits.*

- **The case** is the system in use: who used which tool, on whom, and since when.
- **The lawsuit, complaint, or settlement** is the *evidence* about that case.
- **An allegation is not a finding.** "The complaint alleges…" and "the AG alleged…, which the company denied" are not the same as "it proved."

---

# Pattern 2: Did the Tool Fail, or the People?

This is the live disagreement in several groups. *It's also the line between Tools and Practices.*

- **Tools:** was the output wrong?
- **Practices:** what did people *conclude* from it, who checked, and who signed off?
- A correct output can still lead to a harmful decision. A human "in the loop" counts only if they had the time and information to say no.

<div class="emphasize">

If your group disagrees, **write down both positions** and argue them in the pillars.

</div>

# "The Model Is Proprietary. Is That OK?"

Many groups asked this. **Yes.** You can still meet the standard. Here's how:

- Build Tools from **official descriptions**: court records, settlements, regulator findings, company filings.
- Keep **documented, alleged, and inferred** separate.
- Use research on **similar tools**, and *label it as such*.
- **Say what you looked for and couldn't find**, and who holds it. *That's a finding.*
