# One Rule

On Wednesday, we made decisions about budget cuts in groups with and without Generative AI.

- Folks developed different rationale for making their cuts grounded in their discussions and values
- GenAI systems made cuts differently and, often, didn't agree between groups or group members

> There are numerous examples of where Generative AI (and other automated systems) have made, or been used to make, decisions for people (e.g., Michigan's MiDAS, Australia's Robodebt, Department of Government Efficiency)

# Your Actions Case

This week you add **Actions** and **Critical Literacy** to your DTPA analysis. From the Week 4 materials, a specific claim for Actions names a **decision, a consequence, and a person who instigated it or could have stopped it**:

- **Stated goal vs. outcome** — what was the system for, did it do that, and who was positioned to notice the difference? Include what it achieved, not just what it broke.
- **Who benefited, who paid, who decided** — name all three if you can, and say whether they overlap. The interesting cases are the ones where the decider bore none of the cost.
- **What the output incentivized** — not what the system could do, but what its *existence* made easy, profitable, or safe to do (and costly to refuse).
- **The remedy, and what it would not have caught** — name what actually changed, then state its limit. "Add a human in the loop" is not an answer by itself: *MiDAS's human-reviewed cases were still wrong 44% of the time.*

For **Critical Literacy**, add: how does this case fit existing AI narratives ("solution" vs. "existential threat"), and where does your group land between skepticism and practical judgment?
