# What We Looked For

A scaffold meets the standard when it has:

- **≥750 words** in 1.1–1.4, written in paragraphs
- **≥2 cited sources with specific numbers** in 1.2
- At least one **low-power stakeholder**, plus a **priority paragraph**
- **DTPA paragraphs specific to your case**, not to AI in general
- A **research map** of 4–6 *distinct* sources of ≥2 kinds
- **Every section present**, including References and Team Roles
- **All Roles assigned** and including all group members

# What Went Well

Some moves we want to see more of:

- **Critique vs. constraint:** *"Flock cut its default retention … but that is a company setting, not a law."*
- **Turning a rate into people:** a 1% false-positive rate × 75,000 papers ≈ **750 students**
- **Naming who's invisible:** *"Civilians are largely invisible in this data."*
- **Separating harms:** one rule fixes bad alerts but not a searchable location history
- **Drawing a boundary:** the device should *not* tell someone it's safe to cross the street

# Pattern 1: Sources Didn't Always Hold Up

- **1.1 was uncited almost everywhere**, even though your biggest claims seem to appear there.
- **Research map and References sometimes didn't match** in 6 of 8 scaffolds. *Check your references.*
- **Check that the source says it.** We found:
    - findings credited to papers that don't contain them
    - a reference crediting an author who doesn't exist (it was the *system's* name)
    - incidents retold with the wrong details
- **Who is speaking?** News about a study isn't research. A company selling an AI "humanizer" has a stake in saying detectors fail.

---

# Pattern 2: Numbers Stretched in the Retelling

Small wording changes can change your argument:

- "51.6% **of patients**" → it was 51.6% **of emergency cases**
- "AI as an **alternative**" → the survey asked about AI as an **additional** resource
- "Palantir reported **80,000** users" → **20,000** reported; 80,000 was a researcher's **estimate**

<div class="emphasize">

**For every number: what was measured, on what, and compared to what?**

Sometimes, I think this was because someone didn't review the entire document.

</div>

---

# Pattern 3: Practices and Actions Drifted

- Some Practices and Actions paragraphs described **LLMs or AI in general**, not your case.
- One Practices paragraph **restated the Tools paragraph** in different words.
- **Practices** is about *people and institutions*: who builds, buys, operates, and oversees it, whose labor it depends on, and which rules apply.
- **Actions** is about *what it decides*: who benefits, who pays, and what it rewards.

*Same lesson as Case Study I: these are likely the hardest to research, and yet the most important to get specific.*

# Scaffold 2: Find Your Most Important Lens(es)

---

# One Lens Drives Your Design

Your feedback names **one DTPA component** (sometimes two) as the most central to your case.

- **All four sections** must still meet the standard.
- That lens gets your **deepest analysis** (about 400 words instead of 300).
- Your **design direction (2.5)** should grow out of it.

How we picked the lens(es): **where does the harm or the value actually come from?**

---

# Which Lens?

- **Data:** the failure was stale, missing, or unconsented data
    - *e.g., database records that didn't change; improperly collected or reused data; data that doesn't match the effort*
- **Tools:** the value depends on error rates
    - *e.g., is it better than using a cane, or than guessing "pass" every time?*
- **Practices:** the harm is misuse of access, or no one is accountable
    - *e.g., searches no one authorized; operating outside the legal frameworks that are playing catch up*
- **Actions:** the harm is a decision the system makes
    - *e.g., how urgent is this? Is this score proof of cheating? could a well-trained person do this better?*

---

# Pathway I: What's Already Out There?

- **Survey what exists:** products, apps, and how people do this work today.
- Say what your design does **differently**: cost, focus, privacy, who it serves.
- **Compare to a baseline:** a simple guess, human performance, or the current approach.
- Say what your tool **deliberately does not do**.

*"Existing product, plus more features" isn't a design direction.*

---

# Pathway II: What Does Your Restriction Add?

Most Pathway II proposals **overlap with rules that already exist**:

- state bills and federal laws
- agency directives and procedures
- the company's **own disclaimers and policies**

Scaffold 2 must say:

1. What **exactly** your restriction requires or forbids
2. What it **adds** beyond existing rules
3. **Who enforces it**, and what happens when it's violated

<div class="emphasize">

If the company already says it, it isn't a restriction yet.

</div>
