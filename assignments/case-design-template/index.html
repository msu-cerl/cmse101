Case Design – Assignment Instructions#

Course: AI in the Real World: Data, Power, & Society
Students: Teams of 3‑4
Framework: Data‑Tools‑Practices‑Actions (DTPA)

Important: Review the official case‑design template before you begin (link in the “Resources” section).

1. Overview#

The Case Design project is the capstone of the semester. Your team will produce a fully‑fledged case that applies the DTPA framework to a real‑world AI issue. You may choose one of the two pathways:

PathwayGoalWhat you must deliver
I. Proactive AI SolutionDesign an AI‑driven intervention that tackles a societal problem.• Description of the AI application
• Explanation of how it works (Data, Tools, Practices, Actions)
• Analysis of bias, ethics, and impact
II. Targeted AI RestrictionPropose a safeguard, policy, or limitation on an existing AI system that poses risk.• Identification of the risky AI system
• Rationale for the restriction (evidence‑based)
• DTPA analysis of why the restriction is necessary and how it would be implemented

Both pathways require evidence‑based arguments and APA‑style citations drawn from peer‑reviewed literature, technical reports, news articles, economic data, or historical documentation.

3. Detailed Scaffold Instructions#

Assignment 1 – Scoping Brief (500 words)#

  1. Problem or System Description – Explain the societal issue (Path I) or the AI system you aim to restrict (Path II).
  2. Why it matters – Briefly cite at least two sources showing the significance of the problem or risk.
  3. Preliminary DTPA Lens – Outline one bullet for each DTPA component that you expect to explore.
  4. Research Map – List 4–6 key sources you will use (journal article, report, news story, data set, etc.). Include a placeholder APA citation for each.
  5. Team Roles – Assign a short responsibility (e.g., Data lead, Tools lead, Practices lead, Actions lead, Coordination).

Evaluation: Scope clarity, relevance to course goals, evidence of research planning, and equitable division of labor.

Assignment 2 – DTPA Draft (1 000 words)#

  1. Data – Describe the data set(s) you will rely on, collection methods, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and potential biases.
  2. Tools – Summarize the algorithmic or computational tool(s) (e.g., classifier, LLM, image‑recognition model). Discuss underlying assumptions and technical limits.
  3. Practices – Analyze who creates, curates, and deploys the tool; labor considerations; regulatory or institutional contexts.
  4. Actions – Explain the intended outcomes, who benefits, who may be harmed, and what incentives the AI system creates.
  5. Draft Narrative – Combine the four sections into a cohesive case description (≈ 800 words).
  6. Annotated Bibliography – Provide full APA citations with a 2‑sentence annotation for each source explaining relevance.

Evaluation: Depth of DTPA analysis, quality and relevance of sources, logical coherence, correct APA formatting.

Final Case Design (2 000 words)#

Your final deliverable expands the DTPA Draft into a polished, actionable case:

SectionContent
Executive Summary (≈ 150 words)One‑paragraph overview of the problem, your proposed solution or restriction, and expected impact.
Full DTPA AnalysisExtend each component with concrete evidence, quantitative indicators (where possible), and direct quotes from sources.
Implementation / Policy PlanFor Path I: rollout steps, stakeholder engagement, monitoring metrics.
For Path II: enforcement mechanism, compliance checks, mitigation strategies.
Risk & Ethical AssessmentIdentify remaining uncertainties, potential unintended consequences, and mitigation measures.
ConclusionSynthesize findings and state why your design meets the course’s “critical pragmatism” standard.
ReferencesComplete APA list (no annotations needed).

Evaluation: Comprehensive integration of DTPA, rigor of evidence, feasibility, originality, and professional writing.

Feedback Response Document (500 words)#

After you receive the instructor’s rubric and comments on the final case:

  1. Quote each comment (or reference the comment number).
  2. State the change you made (or explain why you chose not to change it).
  3. Locate the revision (e.g., “Section 3, paragraph 2”).

This document demonstrates reflective practice and shows you can iterate based on scholarly critique.

Quick Checklist for Students#

  • Team formed (3–4 members)
  • Pathway (I or II) chosen and approved
  • Scoping Brief uploaded by Week 4
  • DTPA Draft uploaded by Week 8
  • Final Case Design uploaded by Week 14
  • Feedback Response uploaded by Week 15

Feel free to adjust the due dates to match your semester calendar, but keep the order of milestones (scoping → DTPA draft → final case → response) so that students receive timely feedback and can iterate effectively.
