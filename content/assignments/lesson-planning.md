After the first few weeks of class, groups of students (3–4) can opt in to plan and lead a lesson. Expect this to take each student roughly 10 hours to plan and lead. This is a **deeper engagement** option, not a requirement.

## How to sign up

Reach out to your [instructor](mailto:caball14@msu.edu) **at least two weeks before** your planned lesson. A student may only participate once, and scheduling depends on availability, so your lesson may land later in the semester than you'd like.

Send your completed lesson plan (see the template below) to me at least **five days before** your lesson so I can give you feedback while there's still time to act on it. I will also need time to set up anything in D2L for you. 

> Your group will be exempted from the case study and exit tickets for the days you are leading class.

## What's expected

A lesson you lead should feel like the rest of the course: grounded in the DTPA framework, working toward one of the course's [learning goals](/learning-goals/), and closing with an Exit Ticket like every other class day. Concretely, you need a stated learning goal, a plan for keeping the room engaged, and an Exit Ticket that checks whether the goal landed.

📌 See the [syllabus](/syllabus/#class-engagement) for how leading a class factors into your Class Engagement grade.

## How to Use the Template

Copy the whole template below into a shared document for your team. Work through it in order: the agenda should exist to serve the learning goal, the engagement strategies should exist to run the agenda, and the exit ticket should exist to check the learning goal. Fill in the reflection section yourself, individually, after you've led the class.

## Lesson Plan Template

* **Team members:**
* **Class dates / sessions:** Your group is expected to lead two class days (Monday and Wednesday, typically)
* **Topic or use case:** *(a new case, or a deeper pass on one already in the schedule?)*

### 1. Learning Goal

* What should the class be able to do by the end of this session that they couldn't do at the start?
* Which part of the DTPA framework or the Four Pillars of AI Literacy does this session build toward?

### 2. Agenda

| Time | Activity | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| | | |
| | | |
| | | |
| | | Exit Ticket |

A reasonable split for a single class period: a short hook or recap, a chunk of content or context you deliver, a chunk of small-group or paired work applying it, and a few minutes to close as a full class.

### 3. Engagement Strategies

* How will you get people talking who wouldn't otherwise? (cold-calling, think-pair-share, small groups, a poll, etc.)
* What specific question or prompt will you use to start discussion, and what follow-up will you ask if the room goes quiet?

### 4. Materials

* What should students have already read, watched, or listened to before this class?
* What will you bring: slides, a handout, discussion prompts, a case writeup?

### 5. Exit Ticket

* What question(s) will you ask to check whether your learning goal landed?
* What will you do with the answers: report back next class, adjust your own plan, flag something to me?

### 6. If Things Go Sideways

* If you're running short on time, what's the first thing you cut?
* If you have time left over, what's ready to fill it?

---

### Post-Lesson Reflection (individual, after you lead)

**Required to receive credit (>250 words); due Sunday at 11:59pm instead of your case analyses**

* What went well?
* What would you change if you did this again?
* What surprised you about how the class engaged with your plan?
